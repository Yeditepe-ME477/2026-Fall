(\(\S1.1\)) A Simple 1-D Boundary-Value Problem

  • A boundary-value problem consists of a (linear only, for our purposes and scope) partial differential equation, subject to a dual set of initial and final boundary conditions. The solution of the dependent variable at the final state is sought.
  • \[ u_{,xx} + \mathscr{f} = 0\] (comma stands for differentiating)
  • \[ \mathscr{f}:[0,1] \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \] is a load function mapping in the domain interval.
  • The main constituents to be derived in finite element method are
    • The variational or weak form of the problem, and
    • introduction of the approximate solution space
  • What is the physical relevance of this problem? (longitudinal motion in elastic rod, displacement of a taught string, etc.)
  • What are open and closed domains, \(\Omega\), \(\bar\Omega\), and boundaries, \(\partial\Omega^-\), \(\partial\Omega^+\) in one-d?

(\(\S1.2\)) Strong, or Classical Form

There are two types of boundary conditions

  • Those that are assigned to the displacements (independent variable) \[ u(1) = \mathscr{g} \]
  • Those that are assigned to the displacement derivative \[ -u_{,x}(0) = \mathscr{h} \]
  • The strong form of the boundary-value problem, \((S)\), is stated as follows:

  • Given \(\mathscr{f}:\bar\Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\) and constants \(\mathscr{g}\) and \(\mathscr{h}\), find \(u: \bar\Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\), such that

    \begin{align} u_{,xx} + \mathscr{f} &= 0 \hspace{1em} \textrm{on}\,\Omega \nonumber\\ u(1) &= \mathscr{g} \hspace{1em} \textrm{on}\,\partial\Omega^- \nonumber\\ -u_{,x}(0) &= \mathscr{h} \hspace{1em} \textrm{on}\,\partial\Omega^+ \nonumber \end{align}
  • The solution of the problem is a function \(u(x)\), selected from the space of trial functions, \(\delta\).
  • For numerical suitability of the solution we require that its’ first derivative be square-integrable \[ \int_0^1 (u_{,x})^2 dx < \infty \] Functions that satisfy the above equation are called \(H^1\) functions; indicated as \(u \in H^1\).
  • The other restriction for eligibility to be a member of the trial function set is to satisfy the \(\mathscr{g}\) type boundary condition, i.e. \(u(1)=\mathscr{g}\).
  • The collection of trial solutions, is written as: \[ \delta = \{u|u \in H^1, u(1) =\mathscr{g} \} \]

(\(\S1.3\)) Weak, or Variational Form

  • It is possible to change the form of a boundary-value problem between differential and integral types.
  • For this purpose, we introduce the tool known as weight functions, or variations.
  • This collection is very similar to the trial functions except that we reqire the homogeneous counterpart of the \(\mathscr{g}\) condition. \[ \mathscr{V} = \{w|w \in H^1, w(1) = 0 \} \]
