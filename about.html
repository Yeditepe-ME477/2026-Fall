1. Physics is the Description of Nature

  • As engineers we are usually more interested in how structures behave under loads, thermal conditions, or other physical impetus.
  • But this is not an easy undertaking, especially for arbitrary shapes whose geometry is not an analytical shape (line, circle, etc.)
  • From the 1950s, availability of computers for matrix computations, fueled the effort to convert physical models in the form of boundary-value problems to linear (or nonlinear) systems of equations
    • Finite Differences
    • Finite Element Method
    • Finite Volume Method
    • Boundary Element Method
    • Meshless Methods
    • Iso-geometric Analysis
    • etc.

1.1. Mathematics is the Language of Nature

  • Physical objects and phenomenon are quantified using mathematics. The mathematical toolchest that is useful for this purpose consists of but is not limited to
    • algebra
    • linear algebra
    • calculus
    • integration
    • vector algebra
    • vector calculus
    • differential forms and equations
    • variational forms and equations
    • eigenvalue problems

1.2. Physical Models

  • Try to describe a hollow shell (the Body) that is partly buried in earth (part of its Boundary)
  • How can the condition at the boundary be modeled?
    • There is need for a variable that represents motion of the Body, call it \(\boldsymbol{u}\), the displacement.
  • Let the rest of the free boundary interact with the surrounding fluid.
    • We need another variable to model this interaction, say \(\boldsymbol{t}\), the traction.
  • Of course, there are also fundamental conceptualizations such as coordinates, points, configurations, rotations, etc., required in model building.

2. Finite Element Method is an Expert System

  • But is intelligle and written in a computer language, such as Fortran, C, or Python.
  • The mathematical basis is provided by the theory of continuous media, also known as continuum mechanics, the mechanics of deformable media, etc.
  • In this course, it will be necessary to give a preliminary introduction to continuum mechanics in order to familiarize students with the language used in this expert system.
  • In this course, a modern, free (as in free beer), open-source finite element software will be used throughout the semester, both for illustration and assignments. It is called CalculiX.
